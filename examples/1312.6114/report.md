# Review of: Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes

This report does what a referee does. First it reads the paper: what it claims, whether its numbers agree with each other, what is unsound, unclear or missing, and whether it can be reproduced from what it gives. Then it tries to recompute the results that can be recomputed.

## Summary of the paper

The paper introduces Stochastic Gradient Variational Bayes (SGVB), a reparameterization of the variational lower bound that yields a differentiable unbiased estimator optimizable with stochastic gradients. For i.i.d. datasets with continuous latent variables per datapoint, they propose Auto-Encoding VB (AEVB), which learns a recognition model (encoder) to amortize inference cost, enabling efficient joint learning of generative and variational parameters. The method is demonstrated on MNIST and Frey Face datasets using a variational auto-encoder with a Gaussian approximate posterior. Theoretical advantages are claimed to be reflected in experimental results showing faster convergence and higher lower bounds compared to the wake-sleep algorithm and Monte Carlo EM.

## Minor concerns

**1. The paper states that the estimator variance is small (<1) and omits it, but provides no quantitative variance measurements, error bars, or repeated-run statistics to support this claim.** (evidence)

> The estimator variance was small (<1) and omitted.

Suggested: Report variance of the gradient estimator across multiple runs or minibatches; include error bars in figures and a table of final log-likelihood values with standard deviations.

**2. The experimental comparison only includes wake-sleep and Monte Carlo EM; the wake-sleep algorithm is not described in the main text, and other relevant baselines (e.g., stochastic variational inference with control variates) are cited but not compared.** (completeness)

> We compared performance of AEVB to the wake-sleep algorithm .

Suggested: Provide a clear description of the wake-sleep implementation used; add comparisons with at least one modern stochastic variational inference baseline (e.g., Hoffman et al. 2013 or Ranganath et al. 2013).

## How it stands against related work

The paper introduces the foundational SGVB estimator and AEVB/VAE framework for amortized inference with continuous latent variables. The search results confirm its seminal status: later works explicitly cite it as the basis for the VAE architecture, analyze its regularization properties, extend its encoder flexibility, and modify the wake-sleep baseline it outperforms. The snippets do not reveal the specific SGVB reparameterization derivation or the exact experimental hyperparameters, so the novelty of the gradient estimator itself versus the VAE application cannot be fully disentangled from the snippets alone.

## Questions for the authors

1. Why is wake-sleep described as the only on-line method when stochastic variational inference (Hoffman et al., 2013) is also an on-line method for continuous latent variables and is cited in the paper?
2. Can you provide quantitative results (final log-likelihood values, error bars over multiple random seeds) to substantiate the claimed faster convergence and higher bounds of AEVB?
3. Have you tested the reparameterization trick with non-Gaussian approximate posteriors (e.g., Laplace, Gamma) as suggested in the paper, or is the current implementation limited to the Gaussian diagonal-covariance case?
